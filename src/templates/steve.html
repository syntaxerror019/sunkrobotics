Technical Specifications

Design Philosophy
  • STEVE ROV was engineered with a focus on system simplification, modularity, and reliability.
  • Major redesigns from previous year: removal of Arduino Uno, complete overhaul of camera and networking systems, simplified manipulator.
  • Guiding principles: minimize leak paths, reduce wiring complexity, maximize pilot usability, and ensure maintainability under field conditions.
Frame & Structure
  • Constructed from 6 mm acrylic side panels and 80/20® T‑slotted aluminum profiles for modularity and strength.
  • Clear acrylic end caps laser‑cut in‑house; custom sizing reduced cost and improved fit.
  • Central 100 mm ID acrylic tube (559 mm length) houses electronics; reused from prior year with modifications.
  • Neutral buoyancy targeted by balancing tube displacement (276 in³) with aluminum ballast cubes ("inverse buoyancy modules").
  • Slight negative buoyancy chosen to improve stability during manipulation tasks.
Propulsion
  • Six Blue Robotics T200 thrusters in BlueROV2 configuration.
  • Four thrusters angled at 45° for horizontal translation (X/Y) and yaw.
  • Two vertical thrusters for Z‑axis control.
  • Provides 5‑DOF control (X, Y, Z, roll, yaw) with sufficient redundancy for competition tasks.
  • Custom motor control library maps joystick vectors to thrust allocation matrix.
  • Upgraded tether wiring from 16 AWG to 10 AWG reduced resistive losses, eliminating voltage sag under full load.
  • Thrusters powered directly from 12 V bus via ESCs; capable of full‑speed operation without brownouts.
Manipulator
  • Simplified dual‑hook manipulator fabricated from laser‑cut acrylic.
  • Painted red for underwater visibility.
  • Modular attachment system: hooks mounted via magnets, enabling future tool interchangeability.
  • Optimized spacing for gripping ½‑inch PVC pipes (competition props).
  • Trade‑off: reduced dexterity vs. mechanical gripper, but improved reliability, cost, and ease of maintenance.
Cameras
  • Three modular "tube cameras" provide forward, downward, and manipulator views.
  • Each module includes:
    • Raspberry Pi Camera Module 3.
    • Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W with Ethernet HAT.
    • PLA 3D‑printed tray for mounting.
  • Housed in 50 mm acrylic tubes with SLA resin‑printed flanges (Formlabs Form3, Grey Pro resin).
  • Sealing achieved with 49 mm O‑ring, JB‑Weld epoxy on penetrators, and WELD‑ON acrylic adhesive.
  • One penetrator capped for pressure equalization; second penetrator carries Cat 6 Ethernet + DC power.
  • Mounting: laser‑cut acrylic brackets and custom 3D‑printed angled mounts for optimal pilot visibility.
  • Achieved <200 ms latency and high frame rates, a major improvement over prior year's low‑quality feeds.
Buoyancy
  • Achieved near‑neutral buoyancy using four 5 cm aluminum cubes ("inverse buoyancy modules").
  • Electronics enclosure displaces 276 in³ of water; weights balance displacement.
  • ROV is slightly negatively buoyant, aiding stability during tasks.
Design Philosophy
  • Consolidated electronics into a single enclosure to minimize penetrations and leak risks.
  • Adopted Build, Reuse, Buy philosophy:
    • Build: acrylic housings, trays, mounts.
    • Reuse: thrusters, Raspberry Pis, frame.
    • Buy: precision penetrators, O‑ring flanges.
  • Trade‑offs: accepted heavier tether for reliability; selected larger cameras for robustness.
Electronics Enclosure
  • 100 mm acrylic tube, 559 mm length.
  • Internal tray: laser‑cut 6 mm plywood.
  • Top of tray: Raspberry Pi 4, 12V→5V converter, 12V→9V converter, PWM controller, 5V bus.
  • Bottom of tray: two aluminum U‑bars as 12 V rails, six ESCs.
  • Raspberry Pi communicates with PWM controller via I²C; ESCs driven by PWM.
  • Power distribution:
    • 12 V bus → ESCs.
    • 12 V→5 V converter → Pi 4, Pi Zero 2 Ws, PWM controller.
    • 12 V→9 V converter → Ethernet switch.
Networking & Ethernet Switch
  • Central TP‑Link 8‑port switch, stripped of casing to reduce volume.
  • Housed in SLA resin‑printed waterproof enclosure.
  • Transparent 6 mm acrylic inspection window with silicone gasket.
  • All cables epoxied into housing for watertight integrity.
  • Provides deterministic, low‑latency communication between Pi 4, cameras, and topside.
Tether System
  • Power: 10 AWG high‑strand wires (significant upgrade from 16 AWG).
  • Communication: single Cat 6a Ethernet cable.
  • Slim, low‑drag tether compared to competitors using multi‑cable bundles.
  • Voltage stability maintained under full thruster load.
Buoyancy & Ballast
  • Electronics tube displaces 276 in³ of water, requiring ballast for neutral buoyancy.
  • Four 5 cm aluminum cubes used as "inverse buoyancy modules."
  • Slight negative buoyancy chosen for improved station‑keeping.
Manufacturing Choices
  • Penetrators and O‑ring flanges purchased due to precision requirements.
  • Plan to manufacture O‑ring flanges in‑house in future iterations.
  • External housings (cameras, Ethernet switch, manipulator) designed and fabricated in‑shop, reducing cost.
Design Philosophy
  • Fully custom software stack written in Python for tight hardware integration and low overhead.
  • Avoided ArduSub to eliminate unnecessary abstraction layers and improve performance.
  • Emphasis on modularity, allowing rapid iteration and subsystem upgrades.
Core System
  • Raspberry Pi 4 runs steve‑rov, the central control daemon.
  • Handles:
    • Sensor integration (depth sensor, IMU).
    • Thruster command generation.
    • Camera stream management.
    • Communication with topside client.
Pilot Interface
  • Xbox controller connected to topside laptop.
  • steve‑controller translates joystick inputs into normalized motion vectors.
  • Motion vectors mapped to thruster allocation matrix in steve‑rov.
  • Provides intuitive, low‑latency control with minimal training overhead.
Camera System
  • Each Pi Zero 2 W runs steve‑eye.
  • Encodes video in Motion JPEG, streams via WebSocket.
  • Dedicated compute per camera prevents bottlenecks and ensures high frame rate.
  • Achieved sub‑200 ms end‑to‑end latency, enabling precise manipulation.
Top‑Side Client
  • Linux laptop running lightweight web client (HTML/CSS/JS).
  • Displays three camera feeds simultaneously.
  • Simplified setup: one laptop + one controller, reducing failure points compared to multi‑monitor rigs.
Stability & Positioning
  • Custom PID depth controller implemented in Python.
  • Input: Blue Robotics depth sensor.
  • Control law:
    • Error = target depth – current depth.
    • Output = Kp·e(t) + Ki∫e(t)dt + Kd·de(t)/dt.
  • Gains tuned empirically for minimal overshoot and steady‑state error.
  • Provides "vertical anchor" mode, locking ROV at a fixed depth for precision tasks.
